CERTIFICATE  27570 
 
RESOLVED That the Design Commission, having considered the design of prototypical 
shrouds for 4G and 5G mobile telecommunications equipment for installation on light poles 
citywide, submitted by the Department of Information Technology and Telecommunications 
and the Department of Transportation, represented by exhibits 6673-X & Y of record in this 
matter, hereby gives to the same unanimous preliminary and final approval only to the 
installations on the DOT Octagonal Pole, Type M-2A Signal Pole, Flatbush Avenue Pole, 
Alliance Pole (Type S), TBTA Pole, City Light, and 30’ Davit Pole, including the double-arm 
Davit Pole, but rejects the installations on the Bishop’s Crook and Type-M poles, including the 
double-arm Type-M pole. Approval is given with the understanding that (1) to ensure that 
installations prioritize underserved neighborhoods and the outer boroughs to bridge the digital 
divide, the team will (a) prior to any commencement of each new reservation phase, provide 
details on the total number of poles being released and percentages of distribution across 
each zone, noting that each phase will target greater than 75% of the poles in zones B and C 
and (b) also provide quarterly updates on the status of the installations; (2) the team will submit 
documentation from each community board confirming their review; (3) the team will study the 
inclusion of an integrated label or symbol to indicate to the public that these shrouds contain 
wireless infrastructure; (4) the color of the shrouds will always match the light pole; (5) only one 
set of shrouds will be allowed per pole; (6) if the technology changes during the lifetime of the 
franchise, the boxes will be made smaller and/or the antenna shorter, whenever possible; and 
(7) all sidewalks that are damaged during installation will be replaced-in-kind, per standard 
DOT and PDC review procedure. The Commission reiterates that franchisees should prioritize 
rooftop installations before light pole attachments and that all installations should be 
coordinated with appropriate agencies, including, but not limited to, the Landmarks 
Preservation Commission and Department of Parks and Recreation. Commissioner Nielsen 
abstains. 
 
Final approval is conditioned upon commencement of work before August 10, 2022 and 
submission of comprehensive photographic documentation of the completed project 
consisting of 8” x 10” archival-quality color photographs. 
 
 
A true copy of resolution adopted by 
the Design Commission at its meeting 
on August 10, 2020.
